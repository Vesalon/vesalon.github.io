# About Me

I am a PhD researcher at the **University of New Mexico** in the interdisciplinary **Nanoscience and Microsystems Engineering** program, working within the **Smart Tensors Group** at **Los Alamos National Laboratory**. My work is focused on the intersection of mathematical theory, high-performance computing, and scientific modeling. I find the most fulfillment in **"full-stack" research**: bridging the gap between abstract mathematical underpinnings, efficient numerical optimization, and scalable implementation to solve complex scientific problems.

Currently, my research focuses on **Scientific ML (SciML) and Optimization**, specifically investigating how **neural networks** and **curvature-informed optimization** can be applied to scientific domains. This includes developing **JAX-based second-order optimizers** for inverse problems and implementing **distributed LSQR solvers** using **MPI/NCCL** to improve deep learning wall-time. While my current focus is on SciML, I have a background in information retrieval, recently publishing a **Domain-Specific RAG** architecture at **ICMLA 2024** that has already garnered 35 citations.

Prior to my PhD, I spent five years as a **Senior Machine Learning Engineer** and **Applied Scientist** in industry, where I specialized in taking mathematical prototypes—like **learning-to-rank** and **agentic RAG** systems—into large-scale production. My foundation is a degree in **Discrete Mathematics** from **Georgia Tech**, complemented by research in **additive combinatorics** and competitive success in the **Putnam Mathematical Competition** and **ACM ICPC**.
